Coldwat (fictional)
Overview

Coldwat is a capitalist republic that operates within the framework of social capitalism, but its policies are more closely aligned with pure capitalism. The country is characterized by limited welfare benefits, significant tax cuts for the rich, and a regulatory system that favors profit over public welfare.

Political Structure

Coldwat is an electoral democracy, with a parliament that overwhelmingly supports pro-capitalist parties. Neoliberal ideologies dominate the political environment, with a minor progressive opposition. This results in a parliament that frequently acts as a rubber stamp for policies that favor corporate interests over the needs of the populace.

The regulatory framework in Coldwat is very lax, allowing privatization and deregulation to flourish. This atmosphere has resulted in the deterioration of critical services and welfare systems, contributing to unnecessary deaths and societal instability. The government's claim to be a social capitalist republic contrasts substantially with its citizens' actual realities.

Economic Landscape

Coldwat's economic structure is extremely unequal, with the richest 1% of the population controlling approximately 70% of the nation's wealth. This concentration of wealth exacerbates social inequalities and reduces the possibility of upward mobility for the poor and middle classes.

Recent incidents highlighted the consequences of Coldwat's economic policies, such as massive austerity packs, market deregulation, privatization of most public services, and much more:
  • Heating Crisis: A tragic incident occurred in which 230 people died as a result of exposure to extreme cold caused by the failures of a privatized heating service. The business responsible for it failed to provide auxiliary heating systems for six months after a core failure was discovered. Compounding the problem was a blackout in the national electric infrastructure, which was also privatized, leaving many people without heat throughout the cold arctic winter.
  • Housing Crisis: A housing crisis started as capitalists and progressives attempted to negotiate a merit-based housing system. This compromise stipulates that individuals get higher quality state housing depending on their contributions to society, leaving many without adequate housing options. The progressive faction has strongly opposed this approach, yet the crisis remains unresolved.
  • Healthcare Failures: Coldwat's healthcare system, while supposedly universal, is unreliable and inefficient. An estimated 1,000 to 5,000 people die each year as a result of poor healthcare services and dangerous medical treatments. The rising death toll reveals fundamental flaws in the healthcare system, emphasizing the disconnect between government claims and actual service delivery.
Impending Revolution

The combination of economic disparities, service failings, and escalating death tolls has resulted in significant resentment among Coldwat residents. A democratic socialist revolution appears to imminent as marginalized communities unite to oppose the failing capitalist structures. The mounting frustration marks a potential shift in Coldwat's political environment, as voters demand accountability and reform.
