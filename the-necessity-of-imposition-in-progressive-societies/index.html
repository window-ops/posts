The Necessity of Imposition in Progressive Societies
In the pursuit of socially progressive values, a degree of imposition may be deemed necessary. Without such measures, individuals may not fully embrace the principles required for a progressive society to thrive. Two key aspects are central to this: the imposition of liberal moral values and the development of critical thinking skills.

Imposition of Liberal Moral Values

The foundation of future progressive societies often lies in the principle that all humans are equal. This concept, as outlined in Article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), emphasizes inherent dignity and equal rights for all individuals. However, not everyone inherently supports these values or has the cognitive tools to critically evaluate societal norms. As such, imposing these principles through education becomes crucial for fostering a society where equality and fairness are fundamental.

Teaching Critical Thinking

Critical thinking is another essential component of future progressive societies. It allows individuals to analyze information, question authority, and make informed decisions. In many cases, people lack the autonomy and critical faculties to challenge harmful norms or embrace progressive ideals voluntarily. Therefore, teaching critical thinking from childhood can help mitigate the tendency to blindly follow authority and promote a culture where individuals are empowered to make rational, informed choices.

Challenges in Maintaining Progressivism

Unlike conservatism, which often relies on established norms and authoritarian structures, progressivism requires continuous effort and challenge to the status quo. For progressivism to flourish, educational systems must sometimes supersede parental influence to prevent the perpetuation of unjust or unprogressive values. This can involve integrating progressive values into educational curricula and ensuring that schools embody a more progressive stance than some families might.

Combating the Desire for Domination

While humans are inherently disposed toward fairness, the desire for domination and inequality can also be strong. This conflict necessitates a deliberate effort to emphasize and enforce principles of equality and justice. By teaching that all humans are born equal in dignity and rights, societies can combat the impulses that lead to domination and inequality.

Criticism

The imposition of progressive values can be criticized for leaning towards authoritarianism. By advocating for the enforcement of secular and progressive principles on a society that may not naturally embrace these norms, progressives can be seen as imposing their will without adequate cultural context. Many societies are deeply rooted in non-secular traditions, and attempting to shift these values can be perceived as forcing a minority's beliefs onto the majority.

Moreover, there is the assumption that a minority's vision for a better world is morally superior to the values of the masses. This consequentialist approach, which prioritizes outcomes deemed beneficial by the minority, can conflict with democratic principles and the idea that societal change should be led by broader consensus rather than imposed by a vanguard (progressives).

But if individuals are not truly capable of independent moral judgment, then mild authoritarianism from progressives might be necessary to guide societies toward might be perceived as morally superior outcomes for all. The entire concept can be positioned a form of benevolent paternalism where the enlightened few (those who write the curricula) guide the many toward a supposed better future, which may not actually be desired by the masses.
