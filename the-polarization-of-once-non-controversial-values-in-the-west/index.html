The Polarization of Once-Non-Controversial Values In The West
In the past, a lot of beliefs and moral values, such as including social welfare, internationalism, self-determination, and social/technological progress, were universally accepted and non-political. However, in today's world, specifically in the west, these values have become highly controversial and politicized. This shift is particularly evident in the United States, where political polarization has reached unprecedented levels.

The American Context

The primary focus of political polarization studies often centers on the United States, a country with a binary political system dominated by two major parties: the Democrats and the Republicans. The Democrats, generally considered center-left (while being actually center-right from a european perspective, akin to the EPP and Renew Europe in the European Parliament as of 2024), and the Republicans, being right to far-right (akin to ECR, PfE and ESN in the European Parliament as of 2024), create an apparently stark but false dichotomy in political discourse.

The European Context

In Europe, the political landscape is more complex and diverse, with multiple parties representing a wide range of ideologies. However, the continent is not immune to the forces of polarization. The rise of far-right and nationalist movements, often under the banners of the European Conservatives and Reformists (ECR), Patriots for Europe (PfE) and Europe of Sovereign Nations (ESN), has significantly impacted the political discourse and the acceptance of once-non-controversial values with their reactionary rethoric and politics, capitalizing on the failures of the center-left to provide an alternative to the neoliberal orthodoxy in the EU.

The Role of Social Media and the News

While social media and the news are not the direct cause of political polarization, it acts as a significant amplifier. Social media and news platforms, often owned and controlled by major private companies, seek to divide the public and make them more exploitable. These platforms can echo chambers that reinforce existing beliefs, leading to a more polarized and less nuanced public discourse.

The Uninformed Public and Newton's Third Law of Politics

The general public is often uninformed and operates under a principle akin to Newton's Third Law of Motion: for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. When change is proposed, the public often opposes it, leading to a conservative backlash. This dynamic is a significant driver of political polarization. The more progressives push for change, the more conservatives become reactionary, creating a cycle of increasing polarization.

Ideological Division and Its Impact

Ideological division is a natural part of the political spectrum, but affective polarization can be detrimental to societal cohesion. Reactionary ideologies, which often emerge in response to progressive movements, can further distance people from each other and from progressive ideologies. This can lead to a fragmented society where dialogue and compromise become increasingly difficult.
