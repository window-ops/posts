Prioritizing Social Justice in Environmental Policy
The pursuit of sustainability must be based on a thorough understanding of the social and economic realities confronting marginalized communities (usually low-income and working class), and the idea that technology and green innovation can solve environmental problems on their own is a privileged viewpoint that ignores the struggles of those living in poverty.

We must acknowledge that environmental degradation is often a symptom of underlying injustices such as exploitation, inequality, and a lack of resources. A meaningful transition to sustainability necessitates a multifaceted approach that emphasizes social welfare, economic development, and infrastructure for the most vulnerable communities.

The emphasis on achieving Net Zero by 2050 and other abstract environmental goals must be balanced with a commitment to bringing people out of poverty and promoting social justice. Collective solutions, such as green spaces, public transportation, and social housing, are more important than individual activities or greenwashing products.

Philanthropy and technological innovation are insufficient to handle the scale and complexity of environmental problems, therefore, we must fundamentally restructure our economic and social structures to satisfy people's needs and protect the environment.

The young middle class must not be appeased with superficial solutions; instead, we must ensure sustainability and justice for all, regardless of borders or class. This necessitates a significant change in our approach to environmental policy, one that emphasizes social ecology and the concerns of the most marginalized groups.
