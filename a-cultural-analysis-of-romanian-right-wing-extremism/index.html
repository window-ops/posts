A Cultural Analysis of Romanian Right-Wing Extremism

Romania's contemporary political landscape is marked by the surprising normalization of right-wing extremism, a phenomenon that cannot be fully understood through recent economic or political crises alone. Sociologist Gelu Duminică offers a profound cultural and historical analysis that situates Romanian extremism within deeper patterns dating back to the 19th century and embedded in the fabric of Romanian society. His insights reveal how centuries-old cultural traits, socialization practices, and narratives contribute to the persistence of extremist attitudes today.

Historical and Cultural Foundations of Extremism

Unlike many analyses of Romanian politics that focus predominantly on 20th and 21st-century events, such as communism, post-communist transition and recent austerity measures, Duminică emphasizes the formative role of the 19th century. He argues that the unification of Romanian principalities in 1859 laid the groundwork for a fragmented national identity. Despite sharing language and ethnicity, the regions lacked cohesive shared values, creating fertile soil for division rather than solidarity.

This fragmented identity was compounded by authoritarian socialization under communism, during which strict hierarchical control and authoritarian parenting normalized rigid, intolerant modes of thinking. Far from eradicating extremism, these conditions entrenched a culture where fear and obedience coexisted with latent aggression and social exclusion. The rise of the internet merely made these extremist attitudes more visible, not their origin point.

Cultural Legacy of Violence and Division

Duminică contrasts Romania's cultural trajectory with neighboring countries like Poland, where the 1989 revolution was largely driven by solidarity and collective action. Romanian culture, by contrast with the culture in Poland in the 89s, bears a historical inclination toward bloodshed, conflict, and division, which has normalized extremist rhetoric and violence as tools for managing social tensions.

The persistence of right-wing extremism in Romania is not a new or accidental development but deeply rooted in this cultural legacy. The values and attitudes that justify exclusion, violence, and discrimination have long been embedded in collective memory and practices.

The Role of Romanian Folk Stories

A particularly striking aspect of Duminică's analysis concerns Romanian folk stories as vehicles of socialization. These narratives, among the first cultural materials children absorb, often reinforce stereotypes and normalize violence. Tales that depict Romani people as outsiders or tricksters, or that implicitly or explicitly sanction violence against women, embed discriminatory attitudes early on.

Such stories cultivate a worldview where violence is justified as a response to threats against honor or social order, and where social hierarchies are deeply accepted. This early cultural conditioning shapes childhood beliefs, creating a population more inclined to accept or tolerate extremist ideas, particularly those that target minorities and women.

A Persistent Cultural Justification of Violence

Perhaps most provocatively, Duminică points out that violence against women and minorities is still widely culturally justified in the Romanian community today. This cultural acceptance extends naturally to the normalization of extremist ideologies and violent exclusion. This reality creates a stark divide between the progressive, left-wing audiences who reject such violence and the broader society where these attitudes prevail.

The result is a significant challenge for anti-extremist and left-wing movements. Efforts by independent media and sociologists to present rational, anti-hate messages often preach to the converted and fail to reach or persuade communities for whom such violence and exclusion are culturally ingrained and accepted.

Moving Forward: Cultural Transformation as Key

Therefore, combating right-wing extremism in Romania requires more than economic or political reforms. It calls for a deep cultural transformation that addresses the historical roots of division, the socialization of intolerance, and the narratives that justify violence.

Such transformation could include:

  • Educational reforms emphasizing critical thinking, pluralism, and inclusive histories.
  • Reimagining folk stories and cultural narratives to promote empathy, respect, and solidarity.
  • Engagement with communities and families to build trust and challenge harmful norms from within.
  • Bridging divides between progressive and traditionalist segments of society through empathetic dialogue and social cohesion initiatives.

Understanding Romanian right-wing extremism through Duminică's cultural lens reveals that this is not merely a political or economic problem but a deeply rooted social phenomenon. Any lasting solution must engage the cultural soil that nurtures extremism, transforming it into fertile ground for solidarity, inclusion, and democratic values.
