The Ideologically Neutral Champion of Romanian Democracy (fictional)
Let's get one thing straight: neoliberalism is ideologically neutral. No, really. It's just plain common sense, and that's why it's above petty squabbles about fairness or public interest - especially in Romania, where things have only gotten better since embracing the magic of the free market. Sure, corruption is a problem, but under neoliberalism, corruption is, well, ideologically neutral. That's much better than communism, which was all about dogma, unlike the modern system we have, where the dogma is about, er, not having dogma.

For example, take the PNDL (Programul Național de Dezvoltare Locală) - this revolutionary, non-ideological scheme created under the Dragnea government. The genius was simple: let's make it easier for the private sector to profit from public funds. Who needs complicated European funding rules when we can have a system that's straightforward for those who know how to use it? That's just practical and efficient. And, unlike European funds - which have all those annoying requirements about accountability and results - the PNDL is truly for the people, as long as the people are well-connected.

Neoliberalism is as natural as breathing. For those who still have doubts, just look at the independent press. Outlets like Starea Naţiei, Recorder, and commentators like Silviu Faiăr are always there, questioning and digging into things. But, let's be clear: those are the leftist, progressive channels. The real mainstream press is where you find real neutrality - no ideology, just facts. That's why mainstream politics is all about empowering the most neutral candidates possible.

For instance, we put Dan Voiculescu into power as a Senator. That wasn't ideological - it was a choice, a simple preference, like choosing between two brands of bottled water. What matters is that the system works, as long as we all agree that it does. And if you still aren't convinced, remember: neoliberalism is ideologically neutral. Period. End of discussion. Any other alternative is ideological - and that's a problem, because as we've established, only the current system isn't. It just isn't. Really.

So in conclusion, let's keep it neutral. Because in Romania, when it comes to ideology, the only true neutrality is the kind where we all pretend there isn't any. And that's precisely what makes our democracy so strong - or at least, that's what the mainstream press tells us is the case. But don't take that as a criticism; after all, criticism would be ideological. And we're not ideological here. We're just stating the facts, as long as the facts agree with us.
