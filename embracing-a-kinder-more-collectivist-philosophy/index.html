Embracing a Kinder, More Collectivist Philosophy
In today's capitalist world, we're often encouraged to prioritize individual success, productivity, and intelligence, but this approach is harming our well-being and society as a whole. It's time to rethink our values and consider a more collectivist, humanist, and slower approach to life.

As children, we were naturally more collectivist, curious, and kind. But as we grew older, we were conditioned to prioritize individual achievement and competition. This shift in values has led to a society that celebrates success at any cost, often at the expense of others.

Research suggests that prioritizing kindness and empathy leads to happier people (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10326385, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8652666). In contrast, those pushed to succeed at all costs often struggle with anxiety, depression, and burnout (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4609238). Indigenous communities have long practiced collectivist principles, prioritizing the well-being of the group over individual interests, and have thrived as a result.

Love, not competition, should be the glue that holds our society together. By embracing a more collectivist approach, we can build stronger and more resilient communities that prioritize the well-being of all members. As the Atlantic (https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2019/12/stop-trying-to-raise-successful-kids/600751) put it, "Stop trying to raise successful kids, and start raising kind ones".
