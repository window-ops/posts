Planphlet: A Far-Fetched Plan for Net Zero by 2050 (fictional)
In the book "Planphlet", a group of authors made a plan on how to get net zero emissions by 2050 using technologies that exist and work only in science-fiction.

Chapter 1: CCUNTS

The authors start off their plan with Carbon Capture and Use with New Technologies and Storage (CCUNTS), a magical concept that promises to suck carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. They paint a picture of a world where CCUNTS captures carbon and transforms it into synthetic fuels. However, the reality of CCUNTS is far less enchanting, as it remains a theoretical concept with only a handful of experimental projects. The current state of carbon capture technology is costly and energy-intensive, a detail the authors conveniently overlook.

Chapter 2: BECCS and BECCUNTS

Diving deeper into their plan, the book introduces readers to the wonders of Bio-Energy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS) and its cousin, BECCUNTS, where authors spin a tale of vast monoculture plantations, where magical trees and crops grow rapidly, ready to be burned for energy. They claim that by capturing and storing the resulting CO2, we can achieve negative emissions. Yet, they fail to address the massive environmental impact of such ventures, the threat to food security, and the simple fact that BECCS has never been demonstrated at a meaningful scale.

Chapter 3: Blue Hydrogen

No net zero plan would be complete without the illusion of "clean hydrogen". The authors devote a chapter to this magical fuel, promising a bridge to a great future. They advocate for "blue hydrogen", produced from fossil fuels with carbon capture, neglecting to mention its larger carbon footprint compared to burning coal and gas in the first place. It distracts us from the real, renewable solutions we need.

Chapter 4: Clean Fuels

In the final chapter, the authors present "clean coal", "clean oil", and "clean gas" as reliable energy sources with captured emissions. They even venture into the realm of "negative emissions technologies", describing machines that suck CO2 from the air, despite such technologies being pure science fiction.

In conclusion, "Planphlet" is a journey into a world where achieving net zero emissions is possible with futuristic technologies while continuing to burn fossil fuels. In the real world, betting the future of our planet on unproven and impossible technologies is a recipe for disaster.
