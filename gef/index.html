An International Treaty on Decoloniality, Sovereign Integrity, and Equitable Multilateralism

## Preamble

We, the representatives of sovereign states and peoples,

Recognizing emergent neocolonial vectors including algorithmic financial predation and carbon colonialism,

Reaffirming the primacy of the United Nations Charter (1945), the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966), and the Declaration on the Right to Development (1986),

Acknowledging the systemic inequities perpetuated by predatory lending (as documented by UNCTAD’s Sovereign Debt Workouts), asymmetric trade regimes, and the weaponization of financial institutions,

Concerned by the rise of digital neocolonialism through artificial intelligence governance monopolies and data extractivism,

Emphasizing the urgent need to operationalize the principles of common but differentiated responsibilities (CBDR) in economic and legal systems,

Hereby establish this binding framework to dismantle neocolonial architectures and ensure parity among nations.

## Article 1: Sovereign Equality and Non-Interference

### 1.1 Absolute Sovereign Parity

- Economic parity clause: IMF voting shares, WTO decision-making, and World Bank lending criteria shall be recalibrated to ensure one-state, one-vote proportionality, abolishing veto powers and weighted voting systems.
- Sanctions reform: Unilateral sanctions (e.g., U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control regimes) are prohibited unless authorized by a 2/3 majority of the UN Security Council with no permanent member veto, following due process under Chapter VII of the UN Charter.
- Hybrid Voting Model: Phase-in period (Art 8.1) applies prior to full one-state-one-vote implementation.
- Humanitarian Sanctions Exception: GEC may authorize targeted sanctions through majority vote when crimes against humanity are documented by ICC prosecutors.

### 1.2 Prohibition of Coercive Instruments

- The following instruments shall be prohibited:
-- Secondary sanctions ("third-party" penalties) against non-targeted states.
-- Currency manipulation designated as an act of economic aggression under international law.
-- "Gray zone" coercive measures (e.g., credit rating downgrades targeting specific states).
-- Algorithmic financial blockades through centralized digital currency infrastructure.
-- Enforcement: States imposing unlawful coercion shall face collective countermeasures by GEF signatories, including but not limited to reciprocal financial restrictions.

## Article 2: Anti-Neocolonial Safeguards

### 2.1 Structural Prohibitions

- Debt neutrality: Odious debt originating from prior authoritarian regimes or coercive loan terms shall be subject to automatic nullification upon recommendation by a GEF arbitration panel.
- Intellectual property: TRIPS Agreement flexibilities (e.g., compulsory licensing) are expanded to prevent patent hoarding on essential medicines, green technologies, and food security innovations.
- Digital commons mandate: Source codes for climate adaptation AI systems must be deposited in a UN-managed repository accessible to LDCs.

### 2.2 Decoupling of Institutional Power

- Rotational leadership: IMF Managing Director and World Bank President positions shall alternate between Global North and Global South nationals, elected via transparent UNGA processes.
- Private sector exclusion: Non-state actors (e.g., credit rating agencies, multinational conglomerates) are barred from direct participation in sovereign debt restructuring negotiations.
- Offshore governance: Establish a UN tax convention requiring public registries of ultimate beneficial ownership for all cross-border transactions exceeding $10 million.

## Article 3: Equitable Legal Access

### 3.1 Universal Jurisdiction Reforms

- Legal aid fund: A UN-administered pool financed by 0.1% of Global North states’ military budgets to subsidize Global South legal representation in ICJ, ISDS, and WTO disputes.
- Case prioritization: Disputes involving low-income states shall receive expedited review within international tribunals, with strict 12-month resolution timelines.
- Youth legal observatory: Under-30 jurists from climate-vulnerable states shall constitute 20% of all arbitration tribunals under this framework.

### 3.2 ISDS Abolition

- Replacement mechanism: A Public Interest Review Tribunal under UNCTAD oversight to assess investor claims against states, prioritizing human rights, environmental protections, and labor standards over profit guarantees.
- Digital evidence standards: Blockchain-authenticated documentation shall be prima facie admissible in all GEF-related disputes.
- Adjudication Standards: Tribunal rulings must reference ILO conventions and UNEP guidelines in proportionality assessments.

## Article 4: Economic Institutional Reforms

### 4.1 Opt-Out Protections

- Grace period: States exiting IMF/WTO retain full access to emergency UN liquidity facilities for 5 years, with no credit rating penalties.
- Alternative frameworks: Enabling collective negotiation of South-South trade pacts exempt from most-favored-nation (MFN) obligations when advancing developmental needs.

### 4.2 Conditionality Prohibition

- Debt relief: All structural adjustment mandates (privatization, austerity, deregulation) are replaced with Sustainable Development Compacts co-designed with recipient states.
- Transparency mandate: Loan agreements must publicly disclose all clauses and collateralization terms within 30 days of signing.
- Food sovereignty clause: No loan conditions may require export-oriented agriculture undermining local food security.

- Capacity building: Mandatory technology-sharing clauses in all foreign direct investment (FDI) contracts exceeding $500 million.
- Critical minerals protection: Export taxes on lithium, cobalt, and rare earth elements exempted from WTO challenge for mineral-endowed developing states.

## Article 6: Enforcement Architecture

### 6.1 Global Equity Council (GEC)

- Composition: 33-member body with 22 seats reserved for Global South states, 5 for Small Island Developing States (SIDS), 6 for developed states.
- Powers:
-- Impose compliance bonds on states violating GEF terms, redeemable upon policy correction.
-- Launch emergency trade suspensions against states engaged in economic coercion.
-- Freeze digital asset transfers of non-compliant entities through partnered cryptocurrency exchanges.
-- Establish regional enforcement nodes (e.g., AU, ASEAN) with delegated sanctioning authority.
- Crypto Regulation: Member states must mandate GEC-compliant KYC/AML protocols for domestic crypto exchanges within 24 months of ratification.
- Bond Flexibility: Compliance bonds may be structured as climate-linked securities with maturity extensions for verifiable progress.

### 6.2 Monitoring Mechanisms

- Neocolonialism Index: Annual UNCTAD assessment scoring states on 15 metrics, including tax haven transparency, raw material export dependency, and judicial autonomy.
- Citizen petitions: Individuals and civil society may submit verified evidence of GEF violations directly to the GEC for investigation.
- Algorithmic oversight: Mandate real-time monitoring of cross-border capital flows using AI systems audited by Global South central banks.
- HDI Alignment: Neocolonialism Index shall incorporate tiered scoring aligned with UNDP's Human Development Quartile rankings.

## Final Clauses

# Global Equity Framework (GEF) Draft In Plain English

## Preamble

The GEF is an international treaty that aims to promote decoloniality, sovereign integrity, and equitable multilateralism. It recognizes the need to address neocolonial practices, climate change, and economic inequality.

## Article 1: Sovereign Equality and Non-Interference

- 1.1 Absolute Sovereign Parity: All countries should have an equal say in international economic decision-making, and no country should have veto power. Sanctions should only be imposed with the approval of the UN Security Council.
- 1.2 Prohibition of Coercive Instruments: Countries should not use economic coercion, such as sanctions, to influence other countries. This includes prohibiting secondary sanctions, currency manipulation, and algorithmic financial blockades.

## Article 2: Anti-Neocolonial Safeguards

- 2.1 Structural Prohibitions: Odious debt (debt incurred by authoritarian regimes) should be cancelled, and intellectual property rights should not be used to exploit developing countries. Digital commons (shared digital resources) should be made available to all countries.
- 2.2 Decoupling of Institutional Power: International institutions, such as the IMF and World Bank, should be reformed to give more power to developing countries. Private sector actors should not be allowed to participate in sovereign debt restructuring negotiations.

## Article 3: Equitable Legal Access

- 3.1 Universal Jurisdiction Reforms: A legal aid fund should be established to help developing countries access international justice. Disputes involving developing countries should be prioritized, and young jurists from climate-vulnerable countries should be represented in arbitration tribunals.
- 3.2 ISDS Abolition: The Investor-State Dispute Settlement (ISDS) system should be replaced with a Public Interest Review Tribunal that prioritizes human rights, environmental protection, and labor standards.

## Article 4: Economic Institutional Reforms

- 4.1 Opt-Out Protections: Countries should be able to opt out of IMF and WTO agreements without facing penalties. Alternative frameworks for trade and economic cooperation should be established.
- 4.2 Conditionality Prohibition: IMF and World Bank loans should not come with conditions that undermine a country's sovereignty or development goals.

## Article 5: Sustainable Development Guarantees

- 5.1 Technology Transfer: Green technologies should be made available to developing countries, and research and development should be prioritized.
- 5.2 Industrialization Support: Developing countries should be allowed to protect their industries and implement policies that support their development.

## Article 6: Enforcement Architecture

- 6.1 Global Equity Council (GEC): A new council should be established to oversee the implementation of the GEF treaty. The GEC should have the power to impose sanctions and freeze assets of non-compliant countries.
- 6.2 Monitoring Mechanisms: A Neocolonialism Index should be established to track countries' progress in implementing the treaty. Citizen petitions and algorithmic oversight should be used to monitor compliance.

## Article 7: Reparative Justice

- 7.1 Debt Cancellation: Debt owed by developing countries to multilateral institutions should be cancelled, and climate debt (debt incurred due to climate change) should be accounted for.
- 7.2 Historical Redress: Reparations should be paid to former colonies for historical injustices, and cultural restitution should be made.

## Article 8: Phased Implementation

- 8.1 Voting Reform Transition: The IMF and WTO should transition to a one-state-one-vote system over a 15-year period.
- 8.2 ISDS Replacement Protocol: The Public Interest Review Tribunal should be established to replace the ISDS system.

## Final Clauses

- Ratification: The treaty requires 100 state signatories, including at least 50 from the Global South.
- Reservations: No reservations are permitted on certain articles, and limited opt-outs are allowed for others.
- Review: The treaty should be reviewed every 7 years, and a sunset clause should be established to ensure compliance.
