The United Federation of Classical Liberals (fictional)
The United Federation of Classical Liberals (UFCL) is a country characterized by strict adherence to laissez-faire economics and a political structure that intertwines media influence with governance. While it presents itself as a bastion of classical liberal values, the reality is a complex tapestry of economic disparity, limited political representation, and societal challenges.

The UFCL operates under a parliamentary system, but it is not a true democracy. The parliament is composed exclusively of classical liberals, and the real power lies with the editorial board of The New York Times, whose editorial decisions significantly shape national policy. The parliament serves primarily as a rubber stamp for the newspaper's stances, convening only when it does not take a clear position on an issue.

To be eligible for a parliamentary seat or the presidency, candidates must demonstrate unwavering support for laissez-faire principles. This is assessed through a rigorous process that includes:
  • 50-Question Quiz: Candidates must take a comprehensive quiz multiple times, designed to evaluate their commitment to unregulated markets and classical liberal values.
  • Supervised Assessment: The quiz must be taken under supervision to ensure integrity and honesty.
  • Background Check: An extensive online background check is conducted to identify any anti-market sentiments or affiliations.
The economic model of the UFCL is strictly laissez-faire, characterized by minimal government intervention in the marketplace. While this approach theoretically promotes individual freedom and innovation, it has resulted in significant economic stratification.
  • Wealth Concentration: The top 5% of the population enjoys a vastly superior quality of life, with access to essential services, luxury goods, and opportunities for innovation. In contrast, the remaining 95% struggle to meet their basic needs.
  • Innovation and Investment: Social and technological innovation is predominantly directed towards the affluent, who can afford to invest in new technologies and services, and not to the general public.
The quality of life in the UFCL is very low for the majority of its citizens. Basic necessities such as healthcare, education, and housing are often out of reach for the lower-income population, many to relying on debt to survive.
  • Debt Dependency: Most citizens outside the top tier find struggle to afford essentials.
  • Anarcho-Primitivist Societies: In response to the dire economic conditions, some poorer communities have reverted to anarcho-primitivist lifestyles that prioritize communal living, often shunning modern technology and the capitalist economy altogether.
The United Federation of Classical Liberals aligns itself with international partners based on the editorial stance of The New York Times. For instance, when the newspaper publishes pro-Israel narratives, the UFCL allies with Israel.
