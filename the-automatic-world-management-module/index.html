The Automatic World Management Module (fictional, updated october 2024)
The Automatic World Management Module (AWMM) is a global, open-source AI system that manages Earth's resources efficiently and equitably. It balances supply and demand while considering individual needs and optimizing resource allocation. Unlike traditional economic models, AWMM prioritizes fulfilling genuine demands without straining resources or exacerbating social inequality.

Operated through a network of interconnected supercomputers and utilizing real-time data, AWMM anticipates trends, adapts to changes, and maintains stability in the Resource-Based Economy (RBE). To ensure smooth functioning, it uses NLConsole, a secure, custom operating system with strict security protocols preventing unauthorized access and protecting against malicious activities.

For example, City #84 reports an excess of food while City #45 reports a deficit. After validating these reports through factors like total food supply, population size, and calculated food requirements per capita, the AWMM instructs City #84 to share its surplus with City #45.

Subsequently, as City #45 now has a surplus, it is directed to redistribute the excess to other cities in need. This iterative process continues until all cities achieve a balance between supply and demand, effectively preventing wastage and addressing underprovision issues within the region.

Physical and digital security measures are implemented at data centers housing these supercomputers, including biometric authentication, guards, firewalls, encryption, and regular vulnerability assessments. Such precautions minimize risks and preserve user trust in AWMM, which supports all smart cities within the RBE.

As the backbone of the RBE, AWMM enables essential infrastructure services for participating smart cities. By constantly monitoring, analyzing, and optimizing resources, this innovative technology aims to foster a new era of shared prosperity and sustainability for everyone.

 You know what, screw this. It's like a benevolent dictator, but without the dictator part... or so it seems.

It's based on a top-down approach, where a centralized authority (albeit an AI one) composed of interconnected supercomputers makes decisions for everyone else. Where's the room for community-led decision-making? For people to have a say in how their resources are allocated?

The AWMM is not inherently bad, but we need to be cautious about putting our faith in a system that seems to rely so heavily on technology and top-down control.

The alternative is a direct democracy, where the means of production are collectively owned and automated by computers, who only assist in production and decision-making, but do not replace human judgement.
