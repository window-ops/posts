The Parallel Universe of Europe (fictional)
In a parallel universe, the European Union has undergone a major transformation, shaped by the forces of globalism, neoliberalism, and the far-right. The continent is now a patchwork of economic zones and political enclaves, each with its own peculiarities and contradictions.

The North-Western European Economic Zone (NWEEZ)

The NWEEZ is a sprawling entity that encompasses Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium (minus Brussels), and France. The Social Ecologist Party (SEP), a coalition of social democrats, greens, and neoliberals, governs the NWEEZ. Despite its name, the SEP's environmental policies are superficial and anthropocentric, failing to address the root causes of climate change. Their economic policies, meanwhile, have been a mix of austerity measures and token gestures, leading to widespread disillusionment among the populace.

The far-right has capitalized on this discontent, dominating the media and political discourse. Their rhetoric is a blend of nationalism, anti-immigration, and a twisted form of globalism that seeks to maintain the NWEEZ's economic dominance while closing its borders to the outside world. The Schengen Agreement has been replaced by a series of "mini-Schengens", each designed to keep out NGOs and ensure that 800 billion Euros go exclusively to military spending.

The Nordic Green Left (NGL), a NWEEZ party that draws inspiration from the broader GUE/NGL in the EU, occasionally garners attention through fiery speeches by its newer members. However, their popularity remains low, as their message of genuine social and environmental justice is drowned out by the far-right's populist slogans.

The Greco-European Zone (GEZ)

In the GEZ, comprising Greece, austerity measures have made a return. The economic policies of the past, which were once challenged by figures like Yanis Varoufakis, have been fully reinstated. The Greek people, weary from years of economic hardship, have little hope for change. The far-right, with its promises of national revival and strong leadership, has filled the void left by the failure of progressive politics.

The Orabanite European Zone (OZE)

The OZE, encompassing Hungary, Poland, and Belarus, has embraced a form of extreme right-wing authoritarianism that is both brutal and unyielding. Abortion bans, restrictions on LGBTQ+ rights, and a complete disregard for human life are the hallmarks of this zone. The ruling parties, led by figures like Viktor Orban, have created a society where dissent is met with harsh punishment, and the state's authority is absolute.

The Balkans

The Balkans, located in southeastern Europe, have balkanized once again. The region is a patchwork of small, independent states, each with its own economic and political systems. Romania and Moldova have united and formed an alliance with the United Imperialist Force (The United Imperialist Force is a global alliance between the US, under the second Trump administration, and Russia. It lead to a world that is both multipolar and unipolar at the same time), creating a new power bloc in the region.

Scotland

Scotland has taken a dramatic and unconventional step to rejoin the European Union. After a series of referendums and intense negotiations, Scotland has implemented a series of massive engineering projects that have physically separated it from the United Kingdom. These projects involve huge engines that have cut off Scotland's landmass from the rest of the UK and are slowly moving it closer to the European mainland.

The movement of Scotland has caused significant geological disruptions, leading to frequent and sometimes major earthquakes. These earthquakes, while a source of concern, have become a part of daily life for the Scottish people. However, the inconvenience and disruption caused by these earthquakes are considered minor compared to the broader benefits of rejoining the EU as a fragmented part.

Conclusion

In this parallel universe, Europe is a fragmented and polarized continent, where the forces of globalism and nationalism have created a complex and often contradictory political landscape. The NWEEZ, with its superficial environmentalism and economic austerity, has given rise to a far-right that dominates the discourse. The GEZ and OZE are bastions of extreme right-wing authoritarianism, while the Balkans are a region of small, independent states, each with its own unique challenges and alliances.
