Why We Must Teach Children to Question Eco-Modernism

In debates over climate change and the future of our planet, there is a growing chorus promoting eco-modernism - the belief that technological innovation and market solutions alone can solve the environmental crises we face. While this perspective is often presented as rational and scientific, closer examination reveals it is frequently an emotional impulse dressed up in the language of reason. As concerned people, we have a responsibility to equip the next generation with critical thinking skills early on, so they do not uncritically accept eco-modernist ideology or the neoliberal narratives that underlie it.

The Emotional Roots Behind Eco-Modernism

Statements like "we can solve climate change solely through markets and technology" are less the product of careful analysis and more an expression of hope, fear, and a desire to avoid systemic changes which are uncomfortable to capitalists. High-profile platforms - such as popular science channels like Kurzgesagt - often convey this idea with compelling graphics and confident assertions, creating a seductive narrative of green growth and technological salvation (one of Kurzgesagt's videos on climate, one of Simon Clark's videos on climate). Yet, the evidence tells a different story.

Green growth, the notion that economies can continue expanding indefinitely while reducing environmental harm, has repeatedly failed to materialize in practice. Similarly, ideas like meritocracy and the fairness of the private sector are increasingly recognized as myths rather than realities. These concepts serve particular political and economic interests and should be critically interrogated rather than accepted at face value.

The Urgency of Early Critical Thinking Education

Adolescence is often seen as the prime time to cultivate critical thinking. However, waiting until then risks allowing children to absorb unchallenged ideological messages during their formative years. Early childhood is a critical window for shaping how young minds perceive the world. Teaching children from a young age to question dominant narratives - not just about climate but about power, justice, and equality - is essential to nurturing a generation capable of resisting fascism, technocracy, and other forms of authoritarianism dressed as expertise.

The future demands that children grow up questioning the assumptions behind eco-modernism and neoliberalism, understanding the political and social dimensions embedded in these ideas.

The Reality on the Ground: Climate Change Is Already Here

Climate science is unambiguous: we have likely reached or are nearing a global average temperature increase of 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. Contrary to the often optimistic techno-fix rhetoric, surpassing this threshold will not be a minor inconvenience but a devastating blow with dire humanitarian consequences.

  • Extreme Weather: More frequent and severe natural disasters will disrupt millions of lives (IPCC AR6 (2021), WMO, UNDRR).
  • Sea Level Rise: Rising oceans threaten to displace millions of people, creating climate refugees with nowhere safe to go (IDMC, UNHCR, World Bank Groundswell Report (2018)).
  • Water Scarcity: Between 1 and 4 billion people will face severe water shortages (UN WWDR (2023), UNEP, IPCC AR6).
  • Human Rights Atrocities: Xenophobic policies in wealthier nations may lead to refugees suffering or dying in detention centers or camps (Human Rights Watch, Amnesty International, UNHCR reports).

The Global South and the Limits of Technocratic Solutions

Global South countries face structural hurdles that eco-modernism fails to address:

  • Lack of energy infrastructure to support a renewable transition
  • Shortages of skilled labor to plan and implement renewable projects
  • Dependence on imported technology and materials

Blaming these countries for continuing fossil fuel use ignores the legacy of colonialism and current systemic inequalities. It is neither fair nor effective to impose technocratic solutions without addressing these foundational disparities.

What Needs to Be Done: Bold, Collective Action

If we are serious about combating climate change, incremental market-driven adjustments will not suffice. We need immediate, large-scale public interventions:

  • Nationalize Energy: Developed countries should bring energy systems under public control without the expectation of perfect compensation, prioritizing urgency over profit.
  • Shift to Renewables and Nuclear: Invest exclusively in public-sector projects to accelerate a just and equitable energy transition.
  • Invest in Public Transport: Replace car-centric models with sustainable mobility options to dramatically cut emissions.
  • End Neocolonialism: Developed nations must support the Global South with technology transfer, capacity building, and fair economic partnerships - essentially a new Marshall Plan focused on climate justice.

Protecting Our Children's Minds and Futures

Finally, it is vital to shield children from ideological manipulation - whether from fascist, neoliberal, or eco-modernist sources - while nurturing open, critical inquiry. Popular science channels and media with wide reach have a responsibility to present nuanced, critical perspectives rather than simplistic techno-optimism on deeply human and political issues.

We must demand educational frameworks that empower children to question power structures and advocate for justice and sustainability. Only then can future generations hope to build a truly livable world.
