Internationalism: A Path to Unity and Compassion
In an increasingly interconnected but divided world, internationalism might be a powerful antidote to the reactionary forces of nationalism and xenophobia. By embracing our identity as global citizens, we can foster a sense of unity and shared responsibility, particularly in addressing the struggles of the marginalized. Nationalism, on the other hand, often breeds hatred and division, exacerbating issues like the migration crisis in Europe and fostering xenophobia.

One of the most pressing issues today is the treatment of migrants. Organizations like Frontex, the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, have been criticized by parties like GUE/NGL (left.eu/?s=Frontex) and Greens/EFA (greens-efa.eu/en/article/press/meps-condemns-illegal-pushbacks-and-rejects-frontex-discharge) for their harsh and often inhumane practices. The border wall between Mexico and the United States, an ecological disaster (academic.oup.com/bioscience/article/68/10/740/5057517) and a symbol of exclusion, further compounds these issues. Dismantling these barriers and opening borders can help alleviate the suffering of a lot of people seeking safety and a better life.

The importance of internationalism is furthermore highlighted by the ongoing crisis in the Mediterranean. According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), nearly 100 people have died or disappeared while attempting to cross the central and eastern Mediterranean in early 2024 - more than twice the number for the same period in 2023, which was already the deadliest year for migrants at sea in Europe since 2016 (iom.int/news/iom-chief-nearly-100-disappeared-or-dead-mediterranean-2024-underscoring-need-regular-pathways). This tragic statistic underscores the urgent need for a more compassionate and coordinated response. Everyone should be allowed to rescue migrants at sea, as human life must always be prioritized.

Proletarian internationalism, or better yet, internationalism as a whole, helps us understand that our struggles for social welfare are interconnected, regardless of our nationalities. Whether it is the fight for fair wages, access to healthcare, or the right to a safe and dignified life, these issues transcend borders. Only by working together can we hope to address the pressing challenges of our time, from climate change to economic inequality. And it may even help to solve the migrant crisis we have today.
